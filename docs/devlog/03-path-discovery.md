# Path Discovery
*November 27, 2026*

After suffering across eight LeetCode problems today, I've finally discovered the best way for me to learn.

## Main Idea
I haven't been challenged in a while to wonder how my brain works, until today.

Exploring unfamiliar topics doesn't stop me from working my usual magic with first principles thinking. However, I finally met my match today after stumbling upon concepts I've not only **never** heard of, but which fell outside of my intuition. 

## Gray Area
Usually, I'm thinking about math or physics and interpreting the world with them; that usually takes care of all the hard stuff. You can imagine my surprise when I was stumped by a medium-level puzzle about gray code.

At first, I was discouraged by how stupid I was. It took me more than an hour on average to solve a single problem, and I'd usually give up near the finish line for a hint or two. Looking back, it was honestly a miracle that I was able to solve even two of them by myself.

After likening this experience to my past struggles, I realized my errors in my self-analysis. Just because I couldn't reinvent the wheel every single time didn't mean I was dumb. What mattered was, is, and will always be that I end up wiser.

I love responding to my friends' questions about how I learned something with, "I just taught myself." Seeking out clues or knowledge from external sources doesn't weaken that claim; I kinda understand that now.

The speed with which I learn something when welding my wisdom with others' isn't a mistake—it's a power. I think accepting that is a pretty substantial part of my path to becoming a legendary systems engineer.

Basically, I don't need to reinvent the wheel every time I'm met with a complex problem, but I can build half of it while history fills in for the parts I simply don't know. 

## What This Means For My Work
Whenever my brain accepts this mini epiphany, my efficiency will ***skyrocket***.

Most of the time I spend working on or learning something is spent perfecting my personal understanding, but with that detour—more accurately a mental block—gone, I'll probably crush every personal project deadline.

Does this mean I'll be able to finish adding Orange and Purple to Crusaders on time? Probably not, as per what I discussed in the previous entry.

Fear not, for I will still be working on everything I promised, but slower for the sake of my mental health. 

I'll also be developing a walking simulator with C++ and [Raylib](https://www.raylib.com), simply to sharpen my C++ skills before I really begin my kinetic rigging adventures. Also, I'd like to put out more lore for ***The Ashes***, no matter how few people may see it!

Also, to whoever sees this, I hope you take this personal lesson of mine to heart if you're struggling in a similar way. 

Despite the parts of my life where I've already beaten it, the sickness of self-reliance is still very prevalent within me, but I'm so much closer to curing it. How do I know that though? 

Well, when a person sees the path to defeating something spiritually, they find that it isn't really about eliminating that thing, but accepting and (henceforth) overcoming it. And here I am.
